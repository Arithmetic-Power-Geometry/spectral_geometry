# P108: localized heterogeneous Pauli noise — exact certificate
Date: 2026-10-31
## Theorem
For independent, potentially non-identical single-qubit Pauli channels p_i and q_i, put delta_i=TV(p_i,q_i) and epsilon=1-product_i(1-delta_i). For any ideal-syndrome stabilizer-code ML decoder with strictly positive minimum winner/runner-up coset mass gap gamma under the nominal product channel, 2 epsilon < gamma is sufficient for the same optimal logical-coset decision in every syndrome. The optimal and any fixed decoder's unconditional success probability changes by at most epsilon.
## Proof
At each site take maximal coupling of p_i and q_i with mismatch probability delta_i, independently across sites. The probability of no mismatch is product_i(1-delta_i), so the full product-channel TV is at most epsilon. The probability of any fixed syndrome-coset event changes by at most epsilon. Thus any winner-minus-runner-up gap shrinks by at most 2 epsilon. Positivity ensures all winners remain strict. Success is probability of a union of error strings for a fixed decoder; optimal success is maximum over finite decoder choices. QED.
## Exact tests
Use verified P107 five-qubit coset gaps: mask0 19096819/10000000000; mask1 2647127/2000000000; mask28 13781397/5000000000. For m=1,2,5 affected sites each with delta_i=1/10000, epsilon respectively 1/10000, 19999/100000000, 49990000999950001/100000000000000000000. All 9 cases satisfy 2epsilon<gamma. Computed with Python fractions.Fraction; program at papers/PAPER-04/code/p108_heterogeneous_noise_certificate.py. Note epsilon is a conservative upper bound, not exact product-channel TV in general. The multiplicative improvement over sum_i delta_i is very small at these deltas.
## Critical novelty audit
The coupling/product-TV bound is standard probability, not a novel quantum theorem. Quantum decoder robustness to drift is already investigated in Molavi, Saad, Albarghouthi, Analyzing Decoders for Quantum Error Correction (arXiv:2603.20127, 2026), with polynomial optimization. Cao et al., Quantum Lego Expansion Pack: Enumerators from Tensor Networks, PRX Quantum 5, 030313 (2024), DOI 10.1103/PRXQuantum.5.030313, already provides optimal decoding from generalized enumerators. P108 does not demonstrate computational speedup or tightness against these baselines. No novelty claim.
