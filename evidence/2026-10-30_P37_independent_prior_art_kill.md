CRITICAL PRIOR ART: Anatoly Dymarsky and Alfred Shapere, 'Solutions of Modular Bootstrap Constraints from Quantum Codes', Physical Review Letters 126, 161602 (2021), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.126.161602, https://inspirehep.net/files/f3226a9aed462c94b9d86e77bf5136aa, p.3, explicitly states 'no degeneracies for n <= 6 and one degeneracy for n = 7' comparing refined enumerators of ELC inequivalent graph codes. Their Fig.1 provides the n=7 pair. Johan Henriksson, Ashish Kakkar, Brian McPeak, 'Narain CFTs and quantum codes at higher genus', JHEP 04 (2023) 011, DOI 10.1007/JHEP04(2023)011, https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00025, establishes genus-two distinctions. Together these publications remove the central novelty claim of P36's sharp seven-vertex threshold. A new paper cannot claim to have discovered the first seven-vertex separation.

Methodological caution: PRL's statement is about genus-one refined enumerator degeneracy among physically inequivalent ELC orbits, while P36 compares genus-one/genus-two polynomial fingerprints. The latter's no-split conclusion for n<=6 follows from the stronger absence of physically inequivalent genus-one degeneracy together with known ELC invariance, and published n=7 higher-genus separation supplies sufficiency. This is a synthesis of existing results, not independent physics novelty.

No original normalized physical genus-two Fourier coefficient, no independently established Q1 theorem. Strict NO-GO for a novelty paper built around n=7 threshold. Next research direction should select a genuinely different unresolved question rather than relabeling known results.
