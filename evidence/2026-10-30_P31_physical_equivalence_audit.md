# P31 physical-equivalence and novelty gate

Verified primary literature: Henriksson, Kakkar, McPeak, Narain CFTs and quantum codes at higher genus, JHEP 04 (2023) 011, DOI 10.1007/JHEP04(2023)011, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/JHEP04(2023)011 . It already proves that genus-one-isospectral code CFTs can differ at genus two and supplies the published seven-node pair. Dymarsky and Shapere, Quantum stabilizer codes, lattices, and CFTs, Phys Rev Lett 126 (2021) 161602, accessible https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10274353 , identifies physical T-duality equivalence of graph-code CFTs with edge local complementation (ELC) plus graph relabelling, and distinguishes graph-state local complementation from the physical code-CFT ELC relation.

Rigorous conditional proposition: let C be a set of code descriptions, E the equivalence relation generated by physical T-duality and allowed relabelling, and F:C->N a proposed numerical certificate length. F descends to a function on physical theories C/E if and only if F(c)=F(c') for every generating equivalence edge (c,c'). Proof: necessity follows because every edge is within one equivalence class. Sufficiency follows by concatenating generating edges along any equivalence path, and transitivity of equality. This is a standard quotient-set factorization fact, not a new theorem.

P30's arbitrary linear coordinate change is not itself established as a physical duality. Therefore P30 is a caution against automatic invariance, not a counterexample to ELC invariance. The existing P28-P29 454 value is calculated for one fixed coordinate description, with no verified comparison across ELC-related descriptions. The proposed quantity remains a coordinate-specific combinatorial complexity and cannot yet be called a physical invariant.

Novelty kill: genus-two distinction and ELC physical equivalence already appear in primary literature. No original physical theorem or new normalized partition-function Fourier coefficient has been established. Decision: Q1 NO-GO. Next P32: define exact ELC action on codes, generate a nontrivial ELC-related pair, and test whether the certificate length is constant. Preserve both positive and negative outcomes.
